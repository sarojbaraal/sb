Note: Case study reflecting project control and financial governance operations for a major turnkey telecom rollout from the main equipment vendor perspective.

Context

The Nepal Telecom (NT) GSM 10M Subscriber Expansion Project (Package A & B) was one of the largest national telecom infrastructure projects in Nepal, expanding 2G, 3G, and 4G mobile coverage across millions of new lines. As the primary vendor responsible for turnkey equipment installation, commissioning, and managed services, Huawei managed hundreds of active tower sites across geographically challenging regions (Eastern, Central, Mid-Western, and Far-Western).

As the Project Controller within Huawei's Project Management Office (PMO), the primary responsibility was to maintain tight financial control, ensure accurate revenue recognition according to milestone deliveries, oversee subcontractor cost settlements, and reconcile site completion certificates (PAC/FAC) against commercial contracts.

Problem

Managing large-scale, high-velocity network deployments across hundreds of remote sites created complex project control and cost leakage risks:

Subcontractor Billing Discrepancies: Multiple local Sub-Contractors (LSTs) submitted civil work and installation invoices without standardized site acceptance proof (PAC), causing billing backlogs and vendor payment friction.

Revenue Recognition Delays: Commercial revenue recognition was tied to strict contractual customer acceptance milestones. Delays in gathering site documentation from field engineers delayed cash flow and revenue realization.

Budget vs. Actual Variance Control: Field scope changes (e.g., additional tower heights, power upgrade requirements, extra cable runs) were frequently executed on-site without prior formal Change Order (CO) approval, resulting in unbilled project costs.

Inventory & Logistics Tracking: High-value network equipment (BTS cabinets, microwave dishes, power modules) had to be matched precisely to site dispatch documents to prevent warehouse inventory variance during project audits.

What I did

Implemented Site Milestone Financial Tracker: Designed an integrated project control system linking engineering site completion status (PAC/FAC) with commercial revenue recognition triggers and subcontractor billing milestones.

Subcontractor Cost & Billing Control Framework: Established strict multi-tier verification workflows requiring signed site acceptance documentation before subcontractor invoice processing, eliminating duplicate and unauthorized work claims.

Change Order & Scope Variance Management: Created a Change Request tracking matrix to capture scope variations directly from field teams, securing formal customer approval before vendor implementation to prevent cost leakage.

Project Budget vs. Actual (BvA) Analysis: Performed bi-weekly cost center reviews with Project Managers and Delivery Leads to monitor gross margins, manpower allocation, logistics expenses, and site execution costs.

Audit & Commercial Alignment: Coordinated with Huawei Headquarters Finance and local audit teams to ensure compliance with internal financial guidelines, revenue recognition policies, and tax/import clearance documentation.

Result

Accelerated Subcontractor Billing Cycles: Reduced site documentation verification and payment approval lead time by over 60%, maintaining healthy vendor relations and smooth field operations.

Protected Gross Margins: Mitigated unapproved scope change leakage through strict Change Order logging, protecting project margins across multi-million dollar contract packages.

Zero Financial Audit Non-Conformances: Achieved 100% compliance during internal and external project financial audits for milestone revenue recognition and inventory tracking.

Improved Cash Flow Predictability: Provided senior leadership with accurate weekly project financial forecasting, expenditure projections, and revenue milestone status.

What you can see

The project control framework connects field engineering milestones, customer acceptance certificates (PAC/FAC), supplier invoices, and commercial revenue recognition targets into a single audit-ready management view.
